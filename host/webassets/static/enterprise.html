Simple Host for companies

Give everyone in the company a place to build.

Someone signs in with your company account, describes a page to the AI agent they already use, and it is live at their own address a minute later. No ticket, no repository, no one to ask.

You run it. It is the same Simple Host, with your company sign-in: one service on your own Kubernetes cluster or server, against your own Postgres and your own identity provider. You decide how it is exposed (AI apps can connect only if they can reach it).

What a person gets

An address of their own, and somewhere for a page to keep what it collects.

Their own addresses

Every person gets their own name under your base domain, and every page they publish sits at the root of its own address under it: <site>.<person>.<your domain>. Each page is its own browser origin, so it shares no cookies, storage or scripts with a colleague's pages or with the person's other pages.

Pages that hold data

Key-value, SQLite and file storage, each with rules for who may read and change which rows. Enough for a sign-up form, a poll, a project board that draws its own timeline.

The agent does it

Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, ChatGPT, Claude. They install one skill, or connect to the instance's MCP endpoint by signing in, and reach the everyday operations as tools: publish, roll back, share, manage a team.

Nothing to learn

Signing in is your normal company sign-in. There is no second password, no API key to keep somewhere, and no account for anyone to create or approve.

Who can see what

A new page opens only for the person who made it. They choose who else can: named people, their team, or anyone who can reach the server.

Restricted pages

Name the people who may read it and everyone else gets the same “doesn't exist or isn't shared with you” page as for an address nothing holds (a signed-out browser is asked to sign in first either way). Somebody who should not know it exists cannot tell that it does.

Teams

A team has its own address, and every member can publish and manage its sites with their own sign-in: that is how people work on sites together. Someone outside the team can be made an editor of one site.

Undo and hand-over

A deleted site can be restored for 7 days by default (a setting). A new version can be previewed through a private link before it goes live, and any kept version can be made live again.

Leavers

Your identity provider is asked only at sign-in, so disable a leaver there and here. Suspending an account on the admin page stops its sign-ins, API keys and connected AI apps; an admin can also delete the account and its sites.

What it runs on

Your cluster, your Postgres, your identity provider.

Kubernetes

A Helm chart (and plain manifests): one pod with a persistent volume for the sites, behind your load balancer, which ends TLS. Any conformant cluster, managed or your own.

Postgres

Any Postgres you own, managed or in the cluster.

Your identity provider

Anything that speaks OIDC. Which email domains may sign in at all is yours to set.

Keys and limits

API keys can be publishing-only and expire when idle. Every time and limit (retention, link lifetimes, quotas, rate limits) is a setting.

Nothing that identifies an installation has a default. If a required setting is missing the process will not start, and it tells you which ones.

What it costs

On a Kubernetes cluster you already run, Simple Host adds a share of one node, a small managed Postgres, a disk volume and its traffic.

About 2,000 people

About $70–100 a month on AWS, Azure or Google Cloud, internal only (served over your private network link): about 3.5–5 cents a person.

About 40 people

About $25–45 a month.

What that assumes

List prices, excluding tax, for light to typical use (5 to 20 pages a person a working day), three sites a person, the cluster's existing ingress, and traffic that stays on a private link such as Direct Connect, ExpressRoute or Cloud Interconnect. Serving the internet, a new cluster or heavy traffic cost more; the calculator shows each, line by line.

Upgrading and backing up

Upgrades

The schema is a numbered chain, applied by an init container before the new version serves. Rolling forward is deploying the new image; the migration runs itself.

Backups

Snapshot the sites volume, and your database's point-in-time recovery covers the rest.

Install it

The same Simple Host, from the same repository, with company sign-in turned on.

The setup helper prepares Helm values or Kubernetes YAML for your cluster, with your company’s sign-in. Connect an existing Postgres database or install one in the cluster. It gives you the files and commands, with secrets left for you to fill in locally. No independent penetration test has been done yet: it is a side project built by one person, so review it before you depend on it.
