Your AI app sends the finished site to Simple Host, which puts it online and keeps it there, with versions, a copy to download, and an undo for delete.

Publish a page directlySend the page itself, the HTML and anything alongside it, and it is live in seconds with no build step or upload dialog.

Your AI app sends the files in one request. Text files go in files; images, fonts, and other binaries go base64-encoded in files_base64 in the same request. The site is live at its address as soon as the request returns. A site must include a home page.

Example. A consultant asks their chat app for a one-page rate card with a contact form; the app writes the page and publishes it in the same turn.

Who
Anyone with a finished page, and every AI agent publishing on your behalf.
Where
Whatever your AI does when you say "publish this".
Limits
Script files are refused. Each upload is capped by the per-upload size limit (see Publish a whole folder; Simple Hack allows 25 MB). A site must include a home page.

Publish a whole folderHand over a zipped folder, the output of a normal web build with images and fonts, and the whole thing goes live at once.

A coding agent packages the folder as a tar.gz or zip and sends it in one upload. The upload becomes a new version and is live when it finishes. Example. An agency builds a client's marketing site with a static site generator and has its agent publish the dist folder after each round of changes.

Idle-site cleanup Off by defaultA site unvisited and unchanged for 90 days gets its owner a Keep link by email; 30 days later with nothing done it is deleted.

When the operator turns it on, a site with no visit by a person, no new version, no saved-data or list write, and no other change by its owner for 90 days gets its owner an email with a Keep it link and a link to their page. Keep it resets the clock without signing in. 30 days later with nothing done, the site moves to Recently deleted and a second email carries a Restore it link. The links open a confirmation page and act only on its button, so mail scanners change nothing. Any restore ends the idle state. Keep for good on your sites page, or keep_site from your AI app, marks a site the cleanup never touches.

Example. A developer's three test sites from a hackathon weekend are warned at 90 days; they keep the one they still show to clients and let the others go.

Who
Operators keeping disk for sites people use, and owners with forgotten experiments.
Where
Keep for good under Manage on your sites page, the idle warning with its date on the site's card, or ask your AI app.
Limits
Off by default; the operator turns it on. Sites with a custom domain or claimed name, kept sites, taken-down or offline sites, suspended accounts, preview sites, and admin, event, and reviewer accounts are never warned or removed. At most 50 emails go out per run, removals first. Nothing runs with under 90 days of visit records. All of an account's links stop working when its sign-in email changes.
