Simple Host for companies · a brief

Stateful static websites, on your own infrastructure.

Your people already make prototypes, dashboards and trackers with AI. Simple Host gives each of them an address of their own on your domain to host them, with shared saved data and no server-side code, and gives the company control over all of it.

The problem

Artifacts are everywhere now. ChatGPT, Claude and other AI tools all turn a request into a working page, a dashboard or a small app. But every artifact ends up as a link with a random ID, on a host outside the company. They are hard to find again, hard to tell apart, and nobody owns the list.

What the addresses look like

Each person's work sits under their own name, on your domain, so it reads like a portfolio rather than a list of IDs.

layla-hassan.your-internal-domain.com/sales-dashboard
oliver-bennett.your-internal-domain.com/onboarding-checklist
priya-sharma.your-internal-domain.com/offsite-signup

What it is

An address per person

Everyone gets their own hostname, and each thing they make gets a readable address under it. Colleagues find it by the author's name. People are kept apart from each other. One person's (or team's) sites share their hostname by design; a site shared with specific people gets its own address.

Teams

Teams get an address too, like sales.your-internal-domain.com/pipeline/. Anyone can start a team and add colleagues; every member can publish and manage its sites, and a team closes when its last member leaves.

Pages that keep data

Saved data is shared between the people who can open a page, survives restarts, and works from any browser or phone.

No server-side code

Pages run in the reader's browser. The server stores pages and data and runs nothing that people publish.

Your infrastructure

You run it on your own infrastructure and decide how it's exposed, with your own sign-in: any identity provider that speaks OIDC. AI apps can connect only if they can reach it.

Access

Every page needs a company sign-in, and a new page opens only for the person who made it. They choose who else can open it: named people or teams, anyone in the company with the link, or the company showcase. Opening a page to people who are not signed in takes an admin's approval, or two admins' if you set it that way.

Governance

Admins see every site, who made it, and who can open it. Publishing, sharing and sign-ins are written to a tamper-evident audit log that also streams to your SIEM, and page views to an access log: owners see how many people visited, admins see who.

Leavers and keys

Sign-ins last 8 hours, or 30 minutes idle, by default. One call disables a leaver and ends their sessions, keys and connected AI apps at once. Keys for automation are limited in what they can do, and expire.

Works with your AI apps

People connect ChatGPT, Claude and other AI apps by signing in with their company account, with no keys to copy. A connection lasts 30 days by default, then they sign in again. Your installation serves its own plugin, already pointed at it.

Light to run

One small service, a Postgres database and object storage. Hosting something that already works takes the AI a single short step, so it costs few tokens.

Yours to extend

Your engineers have the code. Add what you need, for example scanning pages for confidential data.

Before you rely on it

Simple Host is a side project built by one person. Its security testing so far is the author's own scripted tests; no independent penetration test has been done yet. Evaluate it the way you would any open-source software: review the code and run your own security review before you depend on it.
