It runs on your own cloud account

Not on ours. You own the server, the bill and the work on it, and you switch it off when the event ends.

The server

The smallest Ubuntu plan any cloud sells. Around five dollars a month, billed by the hour, so a weekend costs cents. Hetzner, DigitalOcean, UpCloud, Vultr, Hostinger and Oracle all work, and UpCloud gives new accounts free credit that covers an event several times over. We are not affiliated with any of them.

The address

Two web addresses, created for you under a domain we run. Nothing to buy and no DNS to edit. Bring your own domain instead if you want your name on it. HTTPS is automatic either way.

The keys

Ask for as many as you need, or hand over a list of email addresses. One key per person, downloadable as a spreadsheet, given out however you already talk to your people.

Afterwards

Ask your agent to tear it down. The server is deleted and the addresses released, so nothing keeps billing and no link is left pointing at a machine somebody else now owns.

Two addresses rather than one is deliberate: what participants publish must never share a browser origin with your dashboard.

Ask me what I want to build, then build it and publish it.

Works in anything that can fetch a link: ChatGPT, Claude, Codex, Copilot, Cursor, Gemini.

They are asked what they want to build, and it is a hackathon, so that is wide open — a page that takes sign-ups, a quiz, a dashboard over a spreadsheet, whatever they can talk an agent into. If a page needs to remember something, it can, without anyone setting up a database.

The key is the account. They are building within a minute of receiving it.

Judging, and the day after

One screen lists every entry with its link, filterable and downloadable as a spreadsheet. That is the list you hand your judges.

Each person can see whether anyone actually visited what they made, split into real people, bots and monitoring.

Everyone keeps their work. Anyone can download their site as a single file at any time, the pages and anything saved in them together, without asking you. Nothing is lost when you delete the server.

Turn on the provider's backups when you create the server, before anyone has built something worth losing. Every cloud on the list will do it for about a fifth of the server price.

One thing to know before judging. Anything a page saves can be changed by anyone who can open that page. The pages themselves can only be changed by the person who owns them. If saved entries count towards a score, have each team take their copy before judging starts.
