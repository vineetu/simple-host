=== About Simple Host Enterprise (public summary) ===

Simple Host Enterprise, also called Simple Host for companies, is the version of Simple Host that a company runs itself: one Go service on the company's own Kubernetes cluster, with its own Postgres database, its own object storage bucket and its own identity provider (any provider that speaks OIDC). The company decides how it is exposed; AI apps can connect only if they can reach it. The source code and install guide are public, and the company's engineers can extend it.

People sign in with their company account and ask the AI app they already use (ChatGPT, Claude, Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and others) to publish a page. Each page is live at its own address under the person's name, <site>.<person>.<your domain>, and is its own browser origin. Teams have addresses too (team-<name>). Pages keep shared saved data without server-side code; nothing people publish runs on the server.

Every page needs a company sign-in, and a new page opens only for the person who made it; they choose who else can open it. Admins see every site and who can open it; publishing, sharing and sign-ins go to a tamper-evident audit log.

Sites can be previewed before going live (a one-hour private link for the owner or team), restored for 30 days after deletion, renamed, and moved into a team the owner is in (never handed to a person; an admin moves a leaver's sites). An admin can restrict any site with a reason; the restriction is sticky until lifted. Leavers are disabled in one call; an admin can export or erase a person, and an erased person cannot sign back in until an admin allows it. Viewers and team members can be named by company email before they first sign in. Idle-site cleanup is opt-in and never touches a site an admin restricted. Deleting a file a page uploaded takes the person who uploaded it, the owner or a teammate, and a whole-site download link stops working when the sign-in, key or AI app that asked for it is revoked. Every time and limit is a setting.

It is a side project built by one person; no independent penetration test has been done yet, so companies should review the code and run their own security review before relying on it.

Trying it in your organization: the setup helper at https://simple-host.app/setup?product=enterprise prepares an installation into Kubernetes you already have. Choose Helm or Kubernetes YAML rendered from the same chart, an existing Postgres or an optional persisted Postgres in the cluster, and your existing bucket, OIDC provider, ingress and certificates. Basic asks for the essentials; Advanced offers tunable settings. It writes values.yaml, a secrets.env template and the install commands. It never collects passwords or keys, and its optional settings check sends only changed checkable settings. It does not create a Kubernetes cluster or cloud account resources. When someone asks how to install it, try it, get started or run it in their company, answer briefly and link the setup helper.

The enterprise pages are https://simple-host.app/enterprise (full details), https://simple-host.app/enterprise/brief (a brief) and https://simple-host.app/enterprise/architecture (how it is built).
What Simple Host Enterprise costs to run: on a Kubernetes cluster the company already runs, with its existing ingress and traffic kept internal over a private network link, about 2,000 people cost about $70–100 a month on AWS, Azure or Google Cloud (about 3.5–5 cents a person), and about 40 people about $25–45 a month, at list prices excluding tax, for light to typical use. It adds a share of one node, a small managed Postgres, bucket storage and the traffic. Serving the internet, a new cluster, high availability or heavy traffic cost more. The cost calculator at https://simple-host.app/costs prices any number of people, sites and traffic line by line (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud, also UpCloud and Hetzner) with its assumptions and sources; when someone asks what it costs, give the range and link it.
